# The vault map, and what Learn does with it

The foundation the vault and learn modules share. Four documents describe
parts of it, each written from inside one part:
[VAULT-SPEC.md](VAULT-SPEC.md) mirrors the notes,
[LEARN-SPEC.md](LEARN-SPEC.md) queues the reading,
[LEARN-GRAPH-SPEC.md](LEARN-GRAPH-SPEC.md) probes what you know, and
[LEARN-MAP-SPEC.md](LEARN-MAP-SPEC.md) gives the procedure that reads a vault
of notes that were never written to be read this way. A fifth,
[LEARN-SOURCES-SPEC.md](LEARN-SOURCES-SPEC.md), landed on main while this one
was being written and supplies material from outside the vault entirely.

This one owns the seam: what the vault produces, what Learn is allowed to do
with it, and the rule that keeps them apart.

Written to [WRITING-GUIDE.md](WRITING-GUIDE.md).

---

## The shape, in one paragraph

Your notes are mirrored, read-only. A **map of the vault** is derived from
them and belongs to the vault: it says what you write about, what you have
said about it, and how much of it there is. Learn reads that map to find out
what you care about, and then treats every one of those things as something
you do not yet know. Knowledge is only ever written by you answering a
question.

```
  the record    1,288 notes, 5.7M characters, in git. One way in, never
      |         written to by anything here
      v
  vault map     themes, and the positions under them. Descriptive, and it
      |         makes no claim at all about what you know
      v
  learn graph   concepts, prerequisites, states, probes. Everything starts
      ^         unknown. Reads the map for topics, your notes for wording
      |
  catalogue     shared, and the only one of the four that is nobody's.
                Wikipedia sections and lecture segments, embedded, supplying
                material about parts of a subject you never wrote a note on
```

The catalogue is the newest of the four and arrived from a different
direction: it was built by the plan while this document was being written.
[LEARN-SOURCES-SPEC.md](LEARN-SOURCES-SPEC.md) owns it. What matters here is
that it is a fourth thing rather than part of either store above it, and that
it carries no `user_id`, because a catalogue of forty thousand Wikipedia
sections is not forty thousand rows per account.

## Why the map makes no knowledge claims

This is the decision everything else follows from, and the earlier drafts of
this document got it wrong.

They tried to read your notes for what you *know*, and landed at a state
called `claimed` meaning "your notes say you have this". Every hard problem in
the design came out of that one move:

- A note generated by a model from your seed idea produced 9% of the
  [75-note trial](trials/2026-09-19-map-75-notes.md)'s nodes, all of them
  marked as positions you hold.
- A copied book chapter would extract thirty or forty clean positions and
  outweigh a dozen of your own idea notes.
- Transcripts and coursework needed a whole routing class to decide whether
  they counted, and that class was throwing away good material.
- Every extracted node had to pass a strict test, because a bad one would
  corrupt a claim about you.

All four dissolve when the map stops assessing you. A copied chapter says you
were interested in that chapter, which is true, cheap to establish and
impossible to get wrong in a damaging way. A note a model wrote for you says
you wanted it written. A transcript says you took the course.

**So the vault map describes, and never assesses.** It is a picture of what is
in your notes. It is allowed to be approximate, because being wrong costs one
tap on a page about your own writing rather than a wrong answer about what you
know.

## The record

Files. Verbatim, one-way from Obsidian, and written by nothing here.
`obsidian.notes` is a mirror of the record rather than the record itself,
which is why a rebuild of it is uninteresting and a loss of it is
recoverable.

Obsidian stays the only writer. The stored PAT is `Contents: Read-only` and
`lib/vault/providers/github.ts` has no method that writes, so this is a
property of the code rather than a rule somebody has to remember. The map
lives in Postgres alongside the mirror and is never written back.

The record is never the unit of meaning. It cannot be: the vault holds a
147,133-character interview-prep file that yields two ideas and an
867-character book note that yields seven. Length predicts nothing about a
note and note type predicts almost everything, measured across a 290-fold
spread of density in the 30-note trial and confirmed on a random sample in
the 75-note trial.

## The vault map

Two levels, and the difference between them is the whole design.

### Themes — what you write about

A subject or recurring concern your notes keep returning to. *Urbanism.* *How
people learn.* *Value and price.* *Turnarounds.*

A theme is not argued with and does not have to pass any test. It is a label
over a body of writing, and the loose bar is the point: "behavioural
economics" is a bad knowledge claim and a perfectly good statement of what you
read about.

| column | |
|---|---|
| `name` | short, for a list |
| `about` | one line on what this covers, in your terms |
| `strength` | how much of the vault is about it, below |
| `first_seen`, `last_seen` | from the notes' own dates, so a cooled interest looks cool |

Themes come out of extraction as per-note topics and are then merged, the same
way positions are: trigram blocking plus batched adjudication. A theme with
one note under it is not yet a theme, and the merge pass is what decides
whether three notes are about one thing.

**Strength is an interest measure, not a knowledge measure.** Its inputs are
how many notes touch the theme, how much text, how recently, and the stance
mix below. A theme you wrote ten thousand words of argument about outranks one
you clipped three articles into, and both outrank one note from 2021.

### Positions — what you said about it

Under each theme, the specific things your notes actually assert. This layer
keeps the strict bar, because a "position" that cannot be argued with is just
the theme restated.

Both tests must pass:

1. Could you write a question that somebody who holds this answers differently
   from somebody who does not?
2. Would being wrong about it cost you anything?

The second test does real work. "NPV quantifies, IRR is comparable" passes the
first and nobody would argue it; a bar that admits facts about tools admits
thousands of them. In the 75-note trial the readers dropped roughly twenty
candidates on this test and none of the 163 that survived read as a shelf
label.

Four kinds, which decide how a position would be probed if it were ever
promoted into a chain:

| kind | example |
|---|---|
| `claim` | Elasticity of supply decides who bears a tax |
| `position` | Single-stair buildings should be legal to six storeys |
| `distinction` | Revealed versus stated preference |
| `frame` | Base rates; five forces |

All four were used in the trial and none collapsed: 47% claim, 37% position,
10% frame, 6% distinction.

### Every position traces to a verified quote

One row per position per note it came from, carrying the note id, the
`blob_sha` that note had when it was read, and the verbatim sentence that
supports it.

**The sentence has to appear in the note body or the candidate is refused.**
This is the locate pass's rule moved one module over. A model that returns a
quote which is not in the text has invented it, and a mechanical check catches
that for free. The trial verified 163 of 163, several with a `strpos()` query
rather than by eye, including one quote carrying the typo `aparments`.

Keying on `blob_sha` is what makes the sweep re-runnable. An unchanged note is
not re-read; a changed one is re-extracted and compared rather than
duplicated. A position whose every supporting quote has left the record is not
deleted, it is marked no longer grounded, because you may well still hold it.

### Stance weights interest, and nothing else

`held`, `encountered`, or `generated`. Your own argument, somebody else's idea
you recorded, or a note a model wrote for you.

Under the old design this was evidence about your beliefs and the third value
was a problem. Here it is an input to `strength` and a filter on the page: all
three mean you were interested, and they differ in how much they are *your
voice*, which matters for reading the map and not at all for what you know.

`generated` is read from Obsidian's callout, not inferred.

### Edges

Six types between positions, each with an optional line saying what the
relation actually is.

| type | means |
|---|---|
| `requires` | you cannot understand B at all without A |
| `supports` | B is true partly because A is |
| `qualifies` | A bounds or conditions B |
| `contradicts` | these two cannot both stand |
| `example-of` | a concrete case of something more abstract |
| `same-as` | one idea under two names |

A seventh has to name the feature that reads it. `concept_mentions` folds in
as a legacy value, closed to new writes, whose consumer is the "brings up"
list already built.

The trial's spread was `supports` 57%, `qualifies` 24%, `example-of` 13%,
`requires` 6%, `contradicts` 1%. `requires` coming out rarest is what the
design wants. `same-as` never fired because Stage 2 only proposes edges inside
one note; it is a merge-time relation.

Only `requires` must be acyclic, and only in the learn graph, where the
frontier walks it. In the vault map nothing walks it, so nothing needs the
constraint.

### Centrality is the ranking

A score per position and per theme for how much else points at it, weighted so
`requires` counts most and `contradicts` not at all. It needs no model call,
it is stable when the map grows, and it is what a zoomed-out view shows.

The abstraction ladder falls out of `example-of` rather than a stored
hierarchy. The trial produced one unprompted: *cities should be built for
people, not cars* sits above *surface parking is among the worst things to
happen to cities*, from two notes that never cite each other.

### Open questions, and tensions

Two smaller things the map records, both of which are the vault's business
rather than Learn's.

**Open questions.** Something a note asks and never answers. The trial's
readers proposed twelve across 75 notes. These are the single best input to
the reading queue, better than any topic, because you wrote the question
yourself.

**Tensions.** Two positions that cannot both stand. The map shows both, where
each came from, and which of six kinds it looks like: scope mismatch, level
mismatch, you changed your mind, a live dispute in the field, you are
inconsistent, or one is simply wrong. Only two of the six are real
contradictions; the rest are clarifications and worth as much.

The app never resolves one. It proposes a crux and you decide, and the
resolution is a new position joined to both by `qualifies`. A dismissal is
permanent: a tension you already waved away coming back is the fastest way to
make the feature look stupid.

**A tension no longer demotes anything**, because nothing in the map is
promoted in the first place. That was a knowledge-state consequence and it is
gone with the knowledge states.

## What Learn does with it

Learn keeps its own graph, in `learn`, and it is unchanged in shape:
concepts, prerequisite edges, goals, `concept_state`, probes, the three-rung
ladder and the information-weighted bar.

What changes is where it starts and what it is allowed to assume.

### Everything starts unknown

No vault material writes a knowledge state. There is no `claimed`, no
inference from having written about something, no credit for a transcript.
A concept enters the graph at `unknown` and moves only when you answer.

The reason is the one this document opens with. A map that credits you with
knowing four hundred things because you once saved an article is wrong in
precisely the place you would rely on it, and the cost of being conservative
is a few extra questions you find easy.

### The map supplies what to offer, not what you know

Three things cross the seam, and nothing else:

1. **A theme becomes something you can choose to learn.** `/learn/next` and
   the Know page rank by the map's `strength`, so what you are offered is what
   you actually write about rather than a topic you typed once.
2. **Your notes under it become context for generation.** When a chain is
   generated for a theme, the positions under that theme go into the prompt so
   the claims use your vocabulary, your examples and the cases you already
   care about. They shape the teaching and earn no credit.
3. **An open question becomes a track** in the reading queue, which is what
   [LEARN-SPEC.md](LEARN-SPEC.md) was built to serve and has never had a good
   source for.

**Nothing crosses back.** Learn never writes to the vault map. A page may join
the two for display, showing that you have been probed on a theme, without
either owning the other.

### Where the catalogue fits, and the one decision it needs

The catalogue supplies what the map cannot: material about parts of a subject
you never wrote a note about. It is retrieval, it is shared, and it belongs to
nobody, so it sits outside this seam entirely.

It does need a decision this document cannot make on its own.
`learn.catalogue_links` points a segment at a concept or a subject in the learn
graph, which was the right target when that spec was written, because the graph
was going to be filled from the vault. It is not any more. On the model here the
graph starts nearly empty and fills a goal at a time, while the vault map holds
about five thousand positions and their themes from the first sweep.

So the candidate targets are the richer store and the sparser one, and the
question is whether a link should point at a vault-map position as well as a
learn concept. Pointing at both is the obvious answer and is not free: it is a
second target column with its own constraint, and it makes "material about this"
a question with two different answers depending on which store you asked from.
Recorded here rather than settled, and named in the open questions below.

### Why this is better than what it replaces

The old design had one graph doing both jobs, which forced the hard call on
every row: is this thing in the graph because he wrote it down or because he
knows it? Splitting them means the vault map can be loose and approximate
where that is cheap, and the learn graph can be strict where it matters,
without either compromising for the other.

It also means the vault sweep no longer touches `learn.concepts` at all, which
is what collapses the build order below.

## Where the schema stands

The earlier draft listed eight disagreements between the specs and the
database, and said all eight had to be settled before the sweep because each
was cheap at forty rows and painful at 1,800. Most of that list existed
because vault-extracted concepts were going to land in `learn.concepts`. They
are not, so it collapses.

**The vault map is new tables in `obsidian`**, alongside the mirror they are
derived from. Greenfield: themes, positions, position edges, the provenance
rows with their verified quote, tensions, and the centrality score. No
migration of existing data, and nothing to get wrong that a later migration
cannot fix cheaply.

**What is left on the learn side** is smaller and none of it blocks the sweep:

| | what | still needed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | subjects are containers, not labels | yes, eventually. No longer urgent: the argument was cross-subject vault concepts, and there are none. 277 references across 45 files |
| 2 | `concept_subjects` has no readers | drop it, trivial |
| 3 | `claimed` state | **no longer wanted.** Nothing vault-derived writes a state |
| 4 | `defend` rung unreachable, `sharp` unwritable | unchanged, and unrelated to this |

Two items are not schema and still gate the work:

- **The privacy policy describes mail ingestion only.** Two paths already send
  note content to a model. This is first, still.
- **The journal exclusion list does not exist.** The one thing here a session
  cannot settle.

### On names

The schema stays `learn`, the notes stay in `obsidian`, and the vault map
joins them there. Renaming buys nothing and costs a sweep of every file that
references them. The repo already has the precedent: the product is called
vault and the schema is not, for a reason written at the head of its migration.

## Build order

1. **The privacy page, and the journal list.** Before another note reaches a
   model.
2. **The vault map tables.** Themes, positions, edges, provenance with the
   verified quote, tensions, centrality. RLS on all of them and the isolation
   test extended before any feature code, which is this repository's rule for
   the sixth time.
3. **One extraction seam.** The node test, the four kinds, the six edge types
   and the quote rule in the shared prompt fragment the chain-writing calls
   already draw on, so a change to what a position is is made once.
4. **The sweep at scale.** A background job, a budget and a resume point,
   which the one-note slice deliberately has none of. Read
   [LEARN-MAP-SPEC.md](LEARN-MAP-SPEC.md) on chunking first: the current
   chunker stops after ten sections, which leaves 40.7% of the text in
   headed notes unread and punishes well-structured notes hardest.
5. **The map, on screen.** `/vault` gains the map: themes by strength, a
   theme's positions, a position's notes and quotes. This is the deliverable,
   and it is worth having whether or not Learn ever reads it.
6. **The merge pass and the review queue.** Themes and positions reconciled
   across notes, then accept, merge or reject, highest centrality first.
7. **Tensions and open questions.** The neighbourhood sweep, the six kinds,
   the proposed crux.
8. **The seam.** Themes rank what Learn offers; a theme's positions go into
   the generation prompt; an open question becomes a track.
9. **The export.** One markdown file per theme, on demand, since the map lives
   in Postgres and nothing writes it back.

Step 1 blocks everything. Steps 2 to 6 are the map and stand on their own.
Steps 7 and 8 are what make it more than a picture of your notes.

### What "it worked" looks like

- 4,500 to 7,000 positions, with themes more than an order of magnitude
  fewer. On 24 September 2026 the live map held 4,934 positions under 241
  themes, drawn from 617 notes. That count comes from the full sweep of 23
  September, taken after every merge made under the old chaining rule was
  undone and re-applied. The sweep left 205 notes unread when it hit the API
  usage limit, and the top of the range allows for them. Under 2,000 means the
  bar is too high and only the obvious is being caught.
- Every position traceable to a note and a verbatim sentence, verified present.
- **Nothing anywhere claiming you know something.** The whole sweep writes
  interest, not knowledge.
- Edge types spread rather than collapsed, `requires` rarest by a wide margin.
- The top twenty themes by strength are recognisable as the things you
  actually think about. No metric catches this and it decides whether the map
  is any good.

## What it costs

Modelled from the 75-note trial's measured classification split and current
Haiku 4.5 pricing, $1.00 and $5.00 per million tokens, at 3.6 characters per
token. The vault is 1,288 notes and 5,724,185 characters, of which 1,039
clear the classify threshold.

| stage | cost |
|---|---|
| classify every note over the threshold | $0.78 |
| extract themes and positions | $2.64 |
| merge, twenty pairs per call | $1.31 |
| cross-note edges and tensions | $1.60 |
| centrality | free |
| **first full build** | **$6.34** |

At the roughly 60% extraction share the evidence-flag change implies, $7.71.

No design choice here should be made on cost. The numbers are modelled rather
than measured, and the spend ledger exists so the first real run replaces this
table with facts.

The one real risk is the merge pass. Trigram blocking plus batched
adjudication removes around 99% of the comparison space and costs nothing but
a few model calls.

**The fallback is now much cheaper than it was when this was written.** The
upgrade, if the merge rate proves bad, is embeddings over the statements, and
the catalogue work has already installed everything that needs: `pgvector` in
`extensions`, `lib/learn/embed/voyage.ts` against the Voyage 4 family at 1,024
dimensions, and an HNSW index as the worked example. So it is a column and a
sweep on an existing path rather than a new vendor and a new decision. Start
with trigram anyway, because it needs no key and no per-row cost, but the
second option is no longer expensive enough to avoid.

## What the sweep must not read

**Journals are excluded by a list you control**, never inferred. Until the
list exists the sweep does not run.

**A note containing a credential-shaped string is skipped and reported**,
never sent to a model. Nine notes currently match `sk-ant-`, `sk-proj-`,
`ghp_` or `AKIA` patterns. The check is a regex over the body before any call,
and the note is recorded as skipped on the sweep run the way an oversized note
already is. Extraction would otherwise post those keys to an API and store
them in the map's evidence quotes.

## Open questions

- **What a catalogue link should point at.** Stated in full above. A segment
  currently links to a learn concept or a subject. The vault map's positions are
  the larger and more personal store, and on this model they exist long before
  the graph does. Decide before the verdict pass writes links at volume, because
  re-targeting them later means re-running it.
- **Whether the course prerequisite prior helps the vault map too.**
  LEARN-SOURCES-SPEC proposes reading a syllabus order as weak evidence for
  `requires`, aimed at the learn graph. The map has the same problem and names
  it as its open risk: most real edges are between positions that never appeared
  in the same note. The same prior would narrow those candidates, and nothing in
  either document says whether it should.
- **What counts as a journal.** Worth deciding by looking at folders rather
  than in the abstract.
- **Whether a theme should ever be authored.** The map is derived, so a theme
  you type has nowhere to live and no notes under it. The case for allowing
  one is an interest you have never written about, which is exactly what the
  reading queue is for. Probably it belongs to Learn as a goal, not to the map.
- **Whether a copied chapter should be extracted at all.** It maps cleanly as
  interest, which is the argument for. It is also someone else's whole
  argument sitting in a personal map, which may read oddly at the top of a
  theme. Decide it after seeing one in the built map.
- **How themes and Learn's subjects relate.** Both are containers over ideas
  and they are derived differently: a theme comes from your notes, a subject
  from a generated chain. Keeping them separate is right for now and they will
  want joining on a screen.
- **Whether tensions are worth the sweep.** They were the most interesting
  thing in both trials and they are also the stage with the least evidence
  behind it. Cheap enough to try, and first to cut if the crux proposals read
  as noise.

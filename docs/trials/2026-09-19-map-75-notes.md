# Trial: 75 notes through Stages 0–3

Run 2026-09-19 against the live vault, which now holds 1,288 notes and
5,724,185 characters. A simple random sample, deterministic
(`md5(id || 'trial75')`), over every note rather than a stratified one, so the
classification split below is the real split rather than a chosen one. Notes
over 6,000 characters were truncated to that length.

Three notes were excluded before sampling, on privacy rather than yield:
`Me/Journal.md`, `Me/Dreams.md` and `Me/Passwords.md`. The journal exclusion
list the map spec relies on still does not exist, so this stands in for it.

**The same caveat as the 30-note trial, for the same reason.** There is no
`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in this environment, so extraction was not run through
Haiku. It was run against the Stage 2 prompt by eight parallel readers, each
given nine or ten notes and the rules from
[KNOWLEDGE-SPEC.md](../KNOWLEDGE-SPEC.md). This tests **the design**, not the
model tier. Nothing below is evidence that Haiku can do it.

---

## The headline

**The node definition holds at random-sample scale, the verbatim quote rule
cost nothing, and the two routing rules are throwing away good material.**

163 candidate nodes from 75 notes. Every one traced to a sentence that really
appears in its note.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| notes sampled | 75 |
| went to extraction (`knowledge` or `mixed`) | 36 (48%) |
| candidate nodes | 163 |
| nodes per note, all notes | 2.17 |
| nodes per extracted note | 4.53 |
| intra-note edges | 106 |
| overlays proposed | 32 (20 interest, 12 question) |
| **evidence quotes verified verbatim** | **163 of 163** |
| orphans (no edge) | 15 of 163, 9% |

Projected across 1,288 notes at 2.17 per note: **roughly 2,800 candidates
before reconciliation.** The 30-note trial projected 2,400. Both sit inside
the spec's 1,500–2,500 bar once cross-note merging is applied, and the
agreement between two differently-drawn samples is the useful part.

## The five questions

### 1. Are the nodes positions, or topics? — **Positions.**

This is what the design rests on and it came out clean again, on a sample
drawn with no stratification to help it. Unedited:

> **Transport cost is land cost.** *(claim, encountered — Order Without
> Design)*

> **Community has a right size.** *(position, held — SEWB Application
> Assignment)*

> **Rate of change, not amount, decides perceived identity.** *(claim, held —
> Ship of Theseus)*

> **Engagement revenue is incompatible with belonging.** *(claim — Small
> Cohort Social)*

Nothing in the 163 reads as a shelf label. The second test added after the
last trial, *would being wrong cost you anything*, did visible work: readers
reported dropping roughly twenty candidates on it, including feature
enumerations, doctrinal lists, and case-specific conclusions about one
company's numbers.

All four kinds were used and none collapsed:

| kind | count | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `claim` | 77 | 47% |
| `position` | 61 | 37% |
| `frame` | 16 | 10% |
| `distinction` | 9 | 6% |

### 2. Does the verbatim quote rule work? — **Yes, and it is free.**

163 of 163 evidence strings were found character-for-character in the note
body. Several readers checked with a `strpos()` query against the stored body
rather than by eye. One matched a quote containing the typo `aparments`,
which is the behaviour the rule wants.

Zero drops for a missing quote means the rule caught nothing here. That is
not an argument for removing it: the check costs one string search, and the
failure it guards against is a fabricated citation, which is the single worst
thing this pipeline could produce. Keep it, and expect it to start earning its
place when a real model runs the extraction.

### 3. Do the edge types mean anything? — **Yes, and `requires` stayed rare.**

| type | count | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `supports` | 60 | 57% |
| `qualifies` | 25 | 24% |
| `example-of` | 14 | 13% |
| `requires` | 6 | 6% |
| `contradicts` | 1 | 1% |
| `same-as` | 0 | 0% |

`requires` came out as the rarest of the types that fired, which is what the
spec asks for and what keeps the frontier thin. The dominance of `supports`
is expected in a vault made mostly of arguments rather than curricula, and it
vindicates adding that type after the last trial: with four types those 60
relations would have been forced into `requires` and `example-of`, and the
cycle trigger would have refused a share of them silently.

`same-as` never firing is a property of the method, not a finding. Stage 2
only proposes edges *within* one note, and a note does not usually state the
same idea twice under two names. It is a Stage 3 and Stage 4 relation.

`contradicts` firing once is consistent with the last trial, which also found
exactly one, in a different note.

### 4. Are the orphans under control? — **Yes. 9%, against a 15% bar.**

Of the 15 orphans, five were the only node in their note and so had no
possible intra-note partner. Excluding those, 6% of nodes came out of a
multi-node note without attaching to anything. Cross-note edges are Stage 4
and were not run here, so the real orphan rate after a full sweep is lower
than 9%.

### 5. Is stance separable? — **Yes, and the ratio is informative.**

99 `held` against 64 `encountered`. The separation ran on the same signals as
last time: first-person markers and argument structure against attribution
and quotation. The ratio moves sharply by note type, which is the useful
part. Pasted job descriptions and book notes skew `encountered`; idea notes
are almost entirely `held`.

## What the top of the map looks like

Cross-note edges were not computed, so this is a reading of the 163 names
rather than a centrality score. It is the check the spec says no metric
catches, and it is the only result here that decides whether the map is worth
building.

Seven clusters account for most of the sample, and two of them are large
enough to be the spine of a map:

**Cities, cars and what sprawl does to people.** Around twenty nodes across
five notes that never cite each other: *Bovadium Fragments*, the *SEWB*
assignment, *Parking lots*, *Order Without Design*, and a note about Dallas.
The abstraction ladder is already visible in the raw candidates, without
anything being asked to build it — *drivers serve the machine* and *parking is
a fiscal loss* sit under *car-centric places atomise society*.

**Right-sized community.** Around eighteen nodes, and the most interesting
result in the trial. *Community has a right size* is stated in the SEWB
assignment about neighbourhoods, and *size networks to cognition* is stated
in *Small Cohort Social* about online groups. Two notes, two domains, one
position. This is the cross-subject node the map spec demoted subjects to
labels in order to keep, and the sample produced one unprompted.

The others: epistemic humility and the limits of knowing (*Laura Fox*, *On
Ways of Thought*, *God is that which cannot be known*); inversion and
avoiding losers (*inversion and asymmetry*, *Inverse Modeling*, *Niche*);
value and price (*The Wealth of Nations*, *South Sea Bubble*, *Utopia for
Realists*); judgement about businesses (*LSME*, *garage*, *campfire*); and
constraint in design (*Shakespeare*, *Game Design*, *Ship of Theseus*).

Whether that is recognisable as the things you actually think about is not
something this document can answer.

## What should change

Five findings. The first two are the substantial ones and both are routing
rules discarding material the node test would have handled correctly, which
is the same failure the last trial found in the folder rules.

### 1. `evidence` is too greedy, and should be a flag rather than a route

Eleven of 75 notes classified `evidence` and produced nothing. Three readers
independently flagged that this costs real positions:

- The MGT 649 Hamilton case carries *"Seems like he got more from history
  than theory. Great Applied Economist"*, a contestable judgement.
- The MGT 430 course summary carries the purpose-culture-strategy framework
  and the social-licence-to-operate idea.
- A job application argues that leaning on a model to write code is now the
  right way to work.

All three are the author's own positions, sitting inside a note whose
*purpose* is to evidence what they were taught or have done. The current rule
reads purpose and routes on it, when purpose and content are independent.

**Change:** `evidence` becomes a boolean on the note, not a fourth class
competing with `knowledge` and `mixed`. A note can be evidence *and* go to
extraction. The classes become `knowledge`, `mixed`, `operational`, with an
`is_evidence` flag alongside. This raises the extraction share and therefore
the cost, which the cost section accounts for.

### 2. The 200-character rule discards the densest notes in the vault

The rule exists to avoid paying to classify 249 stubs, and it is right that
most of them are stubs. But four readers reported it destroying good nodes:

| note | chars | what was lost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Striving itself is Moral Act` | 128 | the most compact genuine position in its batch |
| `Marriage of Heaven and Hell` | 149 | two aphorisms that are arguable positions |
| `Urban Technology` | 146 | city tech should also serve people who want a simpler life |
| `individuation` | 174 | a position, unexamined |

A short note that states one thing plainly is the *best* shape a node can
come from, and the rule is currently biased against exactly that. Of the 228
notes under 200 characters, 61 contain an argumentative verb.

**Change:** drop the free-skip threshold to 80 characters. 130 notes sit
between 80 and 199 and become classify calls. At the measured classify cost
that is a rounding error, and it is the cheapest quality win available.

### 3. Notes written by a model are in the vault, and stance cannot see them

`Pending/Small Cohort Social.md` opens with:

> `> [!note] Created by Claude`
> `> This note was generated and expanded by Claude from a seed idea in [[Ideas]].`

It produced 15 of the 163 nodes, 9% of the sample, and every one was marked
`held` because nothing inside the note attributes the positions elsewhere.
Under the spec as written, a model's expansion of the author's seed idea
enters the map as the author's own position.

`held` and `encountered` do not cover this. A third value is needed —
something like `generated` — set from the callout rather than inferred, and
the map should not count a generated node as evidence of what somebody thinks
until they have said so. This is a gap the spec does not address at all, and
it will only grow.

### 4. Truncation at 6,000 characters is hiding the densest material

Two notes were read at 5% and 12% of their length. The single richest note in
the sample, `Tasks/SEWB Application Assignment.md`, yielded 17 nodes from its
first 6,000 of 54,070 characters, and the class-logistics wrapper hides what
is a sustained argument about why car-dependent development suppresses
contact between strangers.

Stage 1 already chunks on headings, so the real sweep should chunk the whole
note and extract per chunk rather than truncate. The trial truncated for its
own convenience; the pipeline should not.

### 5. Length still predicts nothing

Confirmed again, and more sharply. `The Wealth of Nations` gave 7 nodes from
867 characters. `Niche` gave 6 from 547. `Galaxy Interview Prep` gave 2 from
the 6,000 characters it was shown, of 147,133. The densest notes in the
sample were a 547-character idea note and a course assignment, and the
sparsest were the longest files in the vault.

in `Pending/obsidianvaultmaker project.md` and reported it rather than

`Bulk/Bulk - Ideas/Projects/Coding/World building simulator.md`,
`personal dashboard app.md`, `personal website.md`, `Shopping Manager.md`.

them is the first thing; a secret-shaped-string check in the sweep, which
skips the note and says so rather than sending it to a model, is the second.
Neither is in the spec.

## Cost

Modelled from the measured split and current Haiku 4.5 pricing of $1.00 and
$5.00 per million tokens, at 3.6 characters per token.

| stage | cost |
| --- | --- |
| classify 1,039 notes over 200 chars | $0.78 |
| extract from the 48% that classify `knowledge` or `mixed` | $2.64 |
| reconcile, twenty pairs per call | $1.31 |
| cross-note edges and disagreements | $1.60 |
| centrality | free |
| **first full build** | **$6.34** |

At a 60% extraction share, which is roughly what finding 1 above would
produce, the total is $7.71.

So the spec's "under $6" was close and slightly optimistic. The number is
still small enough that none of the design choices here should be made on
cost, which is the useful conclusion. It remains modelled rather than
measured, and the spend ledger exists so the first real run replaces this
table with facts.

## What this does not tell you

- **Whether Haiku can do it.** Unchanged from the last trial and now the
  single largest open risk. Everything above concerns the design.
- **The cross-note duplicate rate.** Stage 3 was run by reading the 163 names
  rather than by trigram blocking and batched adjudication. Only a handful of
  genuine cross-note duplicates appeared, but 75 random notes rarely cover the
  same ground twice, and at 1,288 the recurring themes are exactly where the
  merge decisions get hard.
- **Whether cross-note edge extraction works.** Stage 4 was not run. Every
  edge here was stated inside a single note, which is the easy case, and the
  same gap the last trial left open.

The French revolution is one of the most famous events in history: explosive, polarizing, and transformative.
Why? We’ll study the history of this event to understand that. Did the revolution advance human rights,
consolidate state power, or both?
We’ll also use the French Revolution to consider the fundamental questions it raises. Why does a regime
collapse? How is a new state built? Why are some revolutions peaceful while others become protracted and
violent? Why was democracy so radical and why does it remain so difficult?
The human drama, too, of this tumultuous decade and a half is irresistible. How did some people –
Maximillan Robespierre, Napoleon Bonaparte, Toussaint L’Ouverture – make and then lose their
extraordinary careers? How did ordinary people take sides?
We have three aims, and they are all related. The first is to help you master the major events and
developments of the revolution itself. The second is to understand what those events meant at the time and
how their importance has been magnified since – how, in other words, they have produced longstanding
political arguments about the conditions for democracy or authoritarianism, the origins of human rights, and
the process of historical change itself. Third, we consider how the revolution has shaped the world, and how
it compares with other revolutions and counter-revolutions, including ones going on right now.
Course goals and learning outcomes: We care about helping you develop specific skills, namely:
-how to read a complex book and assess its arguments;
-how to make arguments and counter-arguments;
-how to approach a historical document;
-how to write clearly and persuasively (more below);
-how to master a (sometimes dizzingly!) complex pattern of facts and events.
